## How to read the costs

Costs are rough 2026 Marin numbers to plan a budget. They are not a bid.
- **Labor:** about $110/hr for a hired carpenter. Work from a ladder at 9 to 11 ft is costed as two people. If Adam does the work as owner-builder, labor is $0, but allow more time.
- **Material:** 2026 retail (big-box store or lumber yard). Simpson parts are ZMAX or HDG.
- **Accuracy:** about ±30%. Engineer, survey and County fees are listed separately.

**Risk tiers:**
- **Tier 1 (stopper):** plan check will not approve, or the inspector cannot sign off, until this is resolved. It usually needs the engineer.
- **Tier 2 (correction):** an expected plan-check comment or failed-inspection item. It is cheap and mostly prescriptive.
- **Tier 3 (housekeeping):** a possible comment, or something an inspector notices on site. It is cheap; do it before the first inspection.

**One rule:** do not close anything up (soffit, fascia, deck boards, backfill) until the inspector or the engineer has seen it. Do not remove the knee braces without the engineer's written OK.

## Tier 1: stoppers

### 1. Setback check (pending the site agent)

**What's wrong or unverified.**
- **The rule:**
  - The R1 rear setback is 20% of the average lot depth: about 19.4 ft from the GIS lines.
  - A deck more than 18" above grade may project 6 ft into it, so the deck must stay at least about **13.4 ft from the rear (east) line**.
  - It must also stay at least 3 ft from the side lines (MCC 22.10.040 Table 2-5 and 22.20.090 Table 3-1).
- **What the GIS shows:**
  - The house is skewed about 20 degrees to the rear line.
  - The County's 2019 footprint, which already includes the original 2018 deck, sits about **14.5 ft** from the rear line: a 1.1 ft margin.
  - The full-depth (15'-4") extension near the north end may be closer, or over the limit.
  - GIS is not a survey, and the research geometry has loose ends. The site agent is finishing this check.
- **Survey trigger:**
  - Marin's Building Inspection Verification procedure (MCC Ch. 22.20) requires a licensed land surveyor's Building Setback Certification when construction is within 1 ft of a required setback on a conventionally zoned lot.
  - The County's site-plan checklist also asks for an accurate boundary survey, and does not accept aerial-photo plans.

**Evidence:** `research/setback_check.jpg`, `setbacks.geojson`, `facts.json` (`setback_check_preliminary`); sketch IMG_5693 (deck outline).

**Cheapest compliant fix.**
1. Get the site agent's number.
2. If the deck clears the limit by more than 1 ft everywhere: no survey is needed for setbacks, but expect Planning to ask how the lines were found.
3. If it is within 1 ft: a boundary survey and the surveyor's Building Setback Certification.
4. If it encroaches: either a Variance (MCC 22.54), or cut the encroaching corner back. A cut-back is structural work and needs the engineer.

Ask the Planning counter before the full submittal. A setback problem can stop the building permit no matter how good the structure is.

**Rough cost and time:**
- $0 if it clears the limit.
- Boundary survey with certification: about $3,000 to $6,000, 1 to 3 weeks.
- Variance: the County Planning fee (not retrieved) plus 2 to 4 months.
- Cut-back: about $3,000 to $10,000.

**Engineer must bless?** No (surveyor). Yes, if a cut-back changes the framing.

**Photograph after:**
- Tape from the deck edge (fascia face) to any found property-line monument at the NE and SE corners.
- The surveyor's stakes, if set.
- File as `O01_NE_setback.jpg`.

### 2. Cantilevered-floor ledger (CRC R507.9.1.2, R507.8)

**What's wrong or unverified.**
- **What the drawings show:** the ledger is bolted to the band of the main floor. Per the house sheets (A-1.10), that floor **cantilevers about 2'-9" past the basement wall** along the whole deck wall.
- **What the photos show:** a soffit runs directly under the ledger.
- **The code:** R507.9.1.2 requires the band joist to bear fully on the primary structure. A cantilever tip that already carries the first-floor wall and roof does not meet that rule as written.
- **The 2018 detail** (3/A2) drew a stud wall under the rim, which is not what is there.

**Evidence:** IMG_5671, IMG_5677, IMG_5678 (soffit under the ledger); `drawings.md` sections 2.2 and 4.1.

**Cheapest compliant fix (in order of cost):**
1. **The engineer checks the cantilevered floor joists** (size, spacing, back-span) for the deck's ledger reaction plus the tie forces, with the wall and roof above. If they pass with a stamped calc, there is no work.
2. **Reinforce at the rim:** for example, sister or block the cantilevered joists at the ledger zone through the soffit, as the engineer details. Cost: $500 to $1,500.
3. **Make the deck self-supporting at the house line:**
   - add a new beam under the joists near the wall on new 6x6 posts and piers;
   - posts on Simpson CBSQ or ABU66Z bases, with CCQ66SDS2.5 / ECCQ66SDS2.5 or BC6 caps per the engineer;
   - the ledger then carries only the ties and nominal load.
   - Excavating near the house footing needs care.

**Rough cost and time:**
- Option 1: $0 (it is in the engineer's fee).
- Option 2: $0.5k to $1.5k, 1 day.
- Option 3: about $6k to $10k: roughly $1.8k to $2.8k material, 4 to 5 days for two people.

**Engineer must bless?** **Yes, always.** The plan checker will see the cantilever on the drawings.

**Photograph after:**
- Before the engineer's visit:
  - the soffit opened at two places (near each end), showing the floor-joist size, spacing and direction, and the rim;
  - the basement wall line below, with the tape across the overhang.
- After any fix: the new blocking or beam, its connectors, and each pier excavation before the pour.

### 4. The second DTT2Z and its anchorage (I06, CRC R507.9.2)

**What's wrong or unverified.**
- **What was seen:** one Simpson DTT2Z, with the stamp legible, on the side of a joist at the ledger. It has (8) SDS screws and a 1/2" rod through the ledger (IMG_5677).
- **Where it is:** in the **southern half** of the deck, about 30 to 35 ft south of the stair. It is probably **not within 24" of either end**.
- **What is unknown:** the house-side end of the rod.
- **What the code wants:** R507.9.2 Fig. (1) needs devices of 1,500 lb or more (DTT2Z: 1,825 lb in DF with 2x framing) **within 24" of each end** of the deck. That means 2 devices minimum, or 4 devices of 750 lb.
- **What the 2018 design called for:** DTT2Z at 4'-0" o.c. and at each end, so the original north bay may already have some. Count them before buying parts.

**Evidence:** IMG_5677 (crop `IMG_5677_dtt2z_lateral_tie.jpg`); group2 issue 1.

**Cheapest compliant fix.** At each end that lacks a device:
1. Install a **DTT2Z** on the side of the first or second joist, within 24" of the deck end, with its (8) 1/4" x 1-1/2" SDS screws (included).
2. Run a **1/2" HDG threaded rod**, or a Simpson **RFB#4** retrofit bolt (HDG), through the ledger and the band, with HDG nuts and washers.
3. Anchor the house side to a floor joist, or to 2x blocking nailed to the floor sheathing, with a second **DTT2Z** (Fig. R507.9.2(1); `product_data/30_*`).
4. **Reach the house side from the exterior soffit** under the cantilever. Open one panel at each end, then patch and paint. This is cheaper than opening interior ceilings.
5. Find out where the existing tie's rod ends while the soffit is open.

The engineer may require more than two ties for a 51 ft deck in SDC D, and must confirm the house-side path on the cantilever (item 2).

**Rough cost and time:**
- Material: about $150 for 2 ends (4 DTT2Z, rod, nuts, blocking), plus about $100 for the soffit patch and paint.
- Labor: 8 to 10 hr, $900 to $1,100.
- **Total: $1.1k to $1.4k.** 1 day.

**Engineer must bless?** **Yes** (lateral system and house-side anchorage).

**Photograph after:**
- At each end:
  - the deck-side DTT2Z with its stamp, all 8 screws, the rod and the nut;
  - the house-side device in the open soffit, before closing;
  - a tape from the deck end to the device, showing 24" or less.

### 6. Post-cap fasteners (F03; CRC R507.5.2)

### 10. Stair pad (K07, D05; CRC R318.7.6, R507.3)

**What's wrong or unverified.**
- **What is there:** the lower flight bears on a **weathered PT plank at grade**, with no concrete seen (IMG_5680).
- **What the 2018 design called for:** a concrete pad 12" or more into competent soil, with #4 bars at 12" each way and the stringers anchored.

## Order of operations (cheapest overall)

1. **This week:**
   - get the setback answer (item 1);
   - pull the County permit history (any 2018 permit?);
   - send the engineer RFP;
   - decide the cedar stair scope (item 18);
   - take down the hanging chair and clear under the deck.
2. **Exposure day (one visit, engineer present):**
   - lift boards at 3 ledger spots;
   - pull the fascia at 2 or 3 posts;
   - open the soffit at both deck ends;
   - dig 2 piers;
   - probe every brace end;
   - photograph every connector;
   - tape every dimension in checklist sections 1 to 9.
3. **The engineer issues the calcs, letter and details.** PCG finalizes the drawings with the NEW items clouded, then submits.
4. **During plan check:**
   - order the parts (DTT2Z, SDS screws, H2.5AZ, flashing, any glass);
   - do the non-structural cleanup (straps, downspout, alarms, fixtures).
5. **After the permit is issued:**
   - corrective work in this order: footing items, then stair pad and supports, ties and braces, guard blocking, then flashing;
   - call each inspection **before** closing anything.
